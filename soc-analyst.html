What SOC Analyst Actually Covers

A SOC analyst's job is mostly about noise: thousands of alerts a day, and the skill is knowing which ones matter. This course is built to train exactly that judgment — not just how SIEM tools work, but how to read a stream of alerts, tell a false positive from a real threat, and escalate correctly under time pressure.

You'll start with networking and Windows/Linux security fundamentals, since log data only makes sense once you understand what normal system behavior looks like. From there you move into SIEM platforms, alert triage, and the MITRE ATT&CK framework analysts use to classify attacker behavior. The course closes with realistic SOC scenarios — the kind of shift you'd actually be dropped into on day one of an L1 role.

A Closer Look Inside the Work

A Live Triage Queue, Mid-Shift

This is the kind of screen you'd actually be working from — an alert dashboard on one side, a log timeline on the other, and a decision to make about what's noise and what needs escalating right now.

  • Read a SIEM dashboard and separate signal from routine noise
  • Follow an alert from first flag through to escalation or close
  • Practice the handoff notes that keep the next shift informed

Why This Field Matters Right Now

Security operations centres are one of the few areas where entry-level hiring has stayed genuinely active, since alert volume keeps growing faster than teams can hire experienced analysts. What separates a hired candidate from the rest usually isn't tool knowledge alone — it's the judgment to triage quickly under a queue of alerts, most of which are noise. That judgment is exactly what repeated, realistic lab practice is meant to build.
