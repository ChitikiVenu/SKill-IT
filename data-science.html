What Data Science Actually Covers

Most of a data scientist's actual time goes into cleaning messy data and asking the right questions of it — the modeling step people picture is usually the smaller part. This course is sequenced to reflect that reality: you'll spend real time on data cleaning and exploratory analysis before machine learning ever enters the picture, because a good model built on bad data is still a bad model.

You'll work through Python, SQL and statistics as the analytical foundation, then move through the full workflow — cleaning, visualization, feature engineering, model building and evaluation — using datasets that look like the messy, real-world data you'd actually be handed on the job, not pre-cleaned classroom examples. Dashboards in Power BI round out the course, since insights that can't be communicated clearly rarely get acted on.

Why This Field Matters Right Now

Organizations are sitting on more data than they know what to do with, and the bottleneck usually isn't collecting more of it — it's having someone who can clean it, ask the right question of it, and communicate the answer clearly enough that someone acts on it. That combination of statistical judgment, technical execution and communication is what this course is built around, rather than treating modeling as the whole job.
